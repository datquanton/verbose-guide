## The one-paragraph thesis

Every house we read lands on the same paradox: **margins are the worst they've been since 2021, yet profits still grow double digits.** The reason is that the earnings engine has changed. NIM expansion no longer drives the P&L — *credit growth quotas* do. The State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) hands out roughly 11–13% growth allowances to most banks, materially more to banks absorbing weak institutions (VPBank has guided for 35% credit growth). Banks holding a bigger quota, cheap CASA deposits and strong capital can grow volume through the margin squeeze; banks without them cannot. That is why 2026 is a **stock-picker's year in Vietnamese banks, not an index year**.

> **Where the houses converge:** long the quota winners with fortress funding — the tickers that appear on multiple buy lists are **ACB, VCB, CTG, HDB and VPB**. The caution list is mid-sized, retail-heavy lenders with thin deposit franchises, where VIS Rating already measures rising delinquencies.

## 2 · The margin story: NIM stays under 3%

The sector's net interest margin fell from **3.5% in 2024 to 2.9% in 2025**, and FiinRatings, KBSV and VIS Rating all expect it to stay below 3% through 2026. The mechanics: 2025 credit growth of 19% ran far ahead of deposit growth of 11.4%, deposit competition returned (headline rates have touched 9%), and funding costs are repricing upward while lending yields are politically and competitively capped.

{{chart:nim}}

For investors the translation is simple: **do not pay for margin recovery in 2026 — pay for funding advantage.** Banks with high CASA ratios, strong capital and access to international wholesale funding (FiinRatings' explicit screen) protect profitability; banks bidding 9% for deposits give the margin away.

## 6 · What could go wrong

1. **Liquidity squeeze intensifies.** LDR at 111–112% with deposit rates touching 9% means funding costs can keep rising into the credit recovery. VNDirect flags mid-2026 as the pressure point. Watch monthly deposit-vs-credit growth prints.
2. **The bad-debt bill arrives.** FiinRatings' key warning: 2025's stable NPL ratio was manufactured by write-offs and recoveries, and provisioning buffers are at their lowest since 2021. If retail delinquencies keep rising, thin-buffer banks re-provision straight out of the 2026 earnings that everyone has already pencilled in.
3. **Mid-sized banks are already cracking.** VIS Rating measured the sharpest 1Q26 deterioration at mid-sized, retail-heavy lenders — rising delinquencies, falling ROAA. The divergence trade cuts both ways: quality compounds, weakness compounds faster.
4. **Quota politics.** The whole 2026 earnings model rests on SBV allocation decisions. A tighter-than-expected quota round, or stricter quarterly enforcement and real-estate caps (already introduced), directly clips the volume that is substituting for margin.
5. **Household leverage.** VIS Rating's structural flag: persistently high rates plus rising household debt raises downside risk for retail-heavy books specifically.

## The bottom line for our investors

> **Core holdings (consensus quality + quota):** ACB and VCB — backed by three houses each, positive Moody's outlooks, fortress funding. CTG adds the public-investment lending kicker.
> **Growth sleeve (higher beta):** VPB — the system's biggest credit quota (35% guided) and a ~22% profit-growth forecast; HDB as the second restructuring-quota play.
> **Own quality, avoid the squeezed middle:** the research is unanimous that mid-sized banks with weak deposit franchises carry the margin, delinquency and funding risk in 2026. If it doesn't have CASA, capital or a quota, it doesn't have a 2026 story.
