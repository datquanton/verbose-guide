## 2. Business & moat

Four engines: corporate lending (historically real-estate-heavy), retail mortgages,
the CASA franchise, and TCBS (the listed brokerage arm, held separately as TCX).

**The moat is CASA, not lending.** Anyone can lend; TCB's excess return comes from
funding cost. At 38.3% CASA it pays less for money than almost any peer, and that gap
*widens* when the SBV drains liquidity — which is the current regime (₫330tn withdrawn
since February, central rate at 25,283). Q2 proved the mechanism: **NIM recovered 3.1%
→ 3.4%** against a near-universal sell-side expectation of compression.

That NIM recovery is the single most important fact in this dossier and it is
under-appreciated. It means TCB's 2026 earnings are not a volume story grinding through
a margin squeeze; they are a funding-franchise story. The screen that follows from this
is CASA ratio and deposit-cost trajectory, not credit quota.

## 5. Earnings quality

Q2 looks clean — the profit came from NII on a recovering margin, not from one-offs or
trading marks. This is the opposite of VPX's position and it deserves credit: TCB's
earnings are the highest-quality in the book. **But note the tension with the July
share-price fall — a record, clean quarter into a falling price is either a market error
or a signal the model is missing. §6 argues it is neither.**

## 6. Valuation — and the finding that changes the picture

The model's TCB exit multiple (8.0× base) is what makes it rank 7th of 8, and the
obvious challenge is the street: **consensus 12-month TP ₫41,828** (range 38,508–48,000;
12 buys, 0 sells). That is +43% against a ₫29,250 price and appears to demolish the
model's +3.6% expected return.

**It does not, and here is why.** KBSV publishes both a TP of ₫42,700 *and* a target
2026 P/B of ~1.1×. TCB currently trades at P/B **1.16×**. A target multiple *below* the
current multiple cannot produce a *+46% price target* on the same share count and book —
it would require book value per share to grow **+53.9%**, impossible for a bank earning
a 15.7% ROE. The two figures are on different share bases.

The reconciliation is the pending **60% bonus issue**, and this is a trap already
documented in `research/guides/data-sources.md` (which names "TCB 60% pending" explicitly
and records that the same lag burned us on HPG and TCX):

| Basis | TP | Adjusted for 60% bonus | vs spot ₫29,250 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consensus average | ₫41,828 | ₫26,142 | **−10.6%** |
| Low | ₫38,508 | ₫24,068 | −17.7% |
| High | ₫48,000 | ₫30,000 | +2.6% |
| KBSV | ₫42,700 | ₫26,688 | −8.8% |

*(₫38,818 implied target BVPS ÷ 1.6 lands within 3.8% of current BVPS ₫25,216 — the
bonus-issue explanation reconciles; nothing else does.)*

**Conclusion: the consensus is not bullish on TCB — it is being read on a stale share
count.** On a consistent basis the street's own target multiple (1.1× P/B) sits *below*
where the stock trades (1.16×). That is marginally **more bearish than my model's 8.0×
P/E base case**, not less.

This matters for the live decision: the "trim TCB to cap" recommendation survives its
single most obvious counter-argument. It also partly explains the price action — a
record quarter into a falling price looks less anomalous once you notice the headline
targets everyone is quoting are ~60% overstated.

**Caveat, held honestly:** I have not confirmed from primary sources whether individual
houses have adjusted. Some may have. The internal inconsistency in KBSV's own two
published figures is strong evidence but it is inference, not a filing. Treated as
**uncertain**; per charter §2 no `exit_pe` or `probs` were changed on this (T4 evidence).

**Pre-mortem — three paths to −40%:** (1) the real-estate de-risking reverses or a
Masterise-complex credit event lands, taking NPL and provisions with it; (2) the NIM
recovery proves to be a one-quarter funding artifact and 2027 consensus resets down;
(3) nothing company-specific — a 35% position simply carries the market down with it,
which is the risk actually being run today.

## 8. Kill criteria

Two are already live in `assumptions.json`; the third is added here:

1. Q3 NIM < 3.25% (the recovery was noise)
2. Developer/related-party NPL formation > 2.0%, or a Masterise bond event
3. **New:** real-estate loan share reverses back above 32% — the de-risking that
   underpins the governance case would be over

## 9. Decision

No change proposed from this dossier. It **corroborates** the standing recommendation
(trim to the 20% cap) by removing the best argument against it. The position is not
being trimmed because TCB is a bad bank — it is the highest-quality earnings stream in
the book — but because 35% in one name is unmanaged risk and, on a correctly adjusted
basis, the price already reflects the quality.
