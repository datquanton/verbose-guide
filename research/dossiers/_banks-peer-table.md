# Bank peer table — are we holding the right three?

**Depth-queue item 14 · 2026-07-27**

The book holds three Vietnamese banks (TCB 35%, VPB 10%, MBB 6.5% — 43.4% ex-broker
look-through). Every dossier so far has asked *is this name good?* This asks the question
none of them did: **are these the best three available?**

---

## 1 · Return earned per unit of book paid

`ROE ÷ (P/B)` — what the shareholder actually earns on the book value they purchase.
Crude, but it is the single ratio that combines quality and price, and it is hard to
argue with.

| Rank | Bank | Held | ROE | P/B | Yield on book paid |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | **MBB** | owned | 20.9% | 1.24 | **16.9%** |
| 2 | **ACB** | **not held** | 17.7% | 1.07 | **16.5%** |
| 3 | VPB | owned | 17.7% | 1.11 | 15.9% |
| 4 | CTG | not held | 18.0% | 1.30 | 13.8% |
| 5 | **TCB** | owned | 15.7% | 1.16 | **13.5%** ← **largest position, 35% of book** |

*Owned = FiinTrade 24-Jul (T3). Not held = broker forecasts (T4) — a tier lower, so the
comparison is indicative, not decisive. VCB omitted: no reliable ROE/P/B pair found at an
acceptable tier, and inventing one would be worse than leaving the row blank.*

## 2 · Two findings

**MBB earns 25% more per unit of book paid than TCB** — and it is the 6.5% position while
TCB is the 35% one. The book's largest holding screens **last of the five** on this
measure. That is now the *third* independent route to the same conclusion:

1. Expected return: TCB ranks 7th of 8 (+3.6%)
2. The TCB dossier: consensus TP is quoted pre-60%-bonus; adjusted it sits ~10.6% below spot
3. This table: worst yield on book paid of five comparable banks

Three different methods, one answer. That is about as much corroboration as this kind of
analysis produces.

**ACB is not held and screens second** — ahead of every owned name except MBB. It also has
the best asset quality on the page: **NPL 0.97% with 114% loan-loss coverage**, against
TCB's 1.15%. And it trades at 1.07× book versus a ~1.5× historical average, a wider
discount to its own history than TCB's.

## 3 · The fair counter-argument

ACB is cheaper for reasons, and they are real:

| | ACB | TCB |
|---|---|---|
| NIM | ~2.8% | **3.4%** |
| Q2 profit growth | ~+5% | **+21.7%** |
| Franchise | conservative, retail/SME | CASA + ecosystem + TCBS |

**ACB is cheaper and lower-growth; TCB is dearer and faster.** That is a genuine
trade-off, not a free lunch, and a growth investor can rationally prefer TCB at these
prices. What is *not* defensible is holding TCB at **35%** — a size that implies the
highest conviction in the book — when it screens worst on this measure, 7th of 8 on
expected return, and its apparent valuation support evaporates once the bonus issue is
handled correctly.

**The finding is about size, not about ownership.** Nothing here says sell TCB. It says a
35% weight is not what the evidence supports.

## 4 · What this does not establish

- VCB is missing entirely — the largest and best-capitalised bank in the system, absent
  from the comparison because no acceptable-tier data was found. That is a hole, not a verdict.
- Not-held names are on T4 broker forecasts; owned names are on T3 terminal data. Comparing
  across tiers is exactly the error `AGENT-CHARTER.md` §2 warns about, so this table
  **cannot** justify a switch on its own.
- ROE ÷ P/B ignores growth, capital adequacy, funding structure and governance. MBB's
  ROE is *falling* (25.0 → 20.9%) while ACB's is stable — a fact this ratio hides and the
  MBB screen already flagged.

**No model input changed.** This is a screening exercise, and a switch recommendation would
need ACB taken through S1–S3 properly.

## 4b · Update, 2026-07-27 12:53 — ACB has since filed Q2

This table was built at 03:53 using an ACB ROE of 17.7%, which was a **broker forecast**.
ACB filed its Q2 statement later the same day. Net interest income rose **16.5%** but
pre-tax profit **fell ~12%**, because provisions were raised sharply. First-half profit is
**48% of the full-year forecast**, so the year remains on pace and the ROE driving the
ranking is not yet broken.

**What changes.** ACB is no longer simply "cheaper with the best asset quality." It is a
bank choosing to spend a growing income stream on provisions. That is either prudence,
which would support the ranking, or an early read on credit quality that the headline NPL
of 0.97% does not yet show. **The S1 screen recommended below must resolve which, before
any switch is contemplated.** The recommendation to add ACB to the universe stands; the
implicit "and it looks better than TCB" does not, until this is understood.

## 5 · Recommendation

1. **Add ACB to the research universe** and run an S1 screen. It screens second of five on
   the one ratio that combines quality and price, has the best asset quality on the page,
   and is not owned. That is the definition of a candidate.
2. Treat this as **corroboration for the TCB trim**, not as a new reason for it — the trim
   already stands on two independent legs.
3. Fill the **VCB** gap when terminal-tier data is available.
