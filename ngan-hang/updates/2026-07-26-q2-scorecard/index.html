Vietnam Banks, Half-Time 2026: Grading the Forecasts Against the Actual Q2 Prints

In December, ten research houses told us Vietnamese banks would grow profits 16–20% in 2026 on falling margins. The first-half numbers are now filed. We scored the forecasts — who was right, who was right for the wrong reason, and what the second half actually hinges on.

The one-paragraph thesis

Six months ago the consensus was unusually tight: profits up 16–20%, margins the worst since 2021, growth coming from credit quota rather than spread. Half the year is now filed, and the consensus was directionally right and mechanically wrong. Profits did grow — Techcombank +21.7% in Q2, VPBank +77.7% — but at Techcombank the margin recovered, from 3.1% to 3.4%, which is the opposite of what almost every house modelled. The banks are not grinding out volume growth through a margin squeeze. Some of them are getting their margins back.

What changed in our view: we now think the 2026 bank story is less about who received the biggest quota and more about who funds themselves cheaply enough to hold margin while deploying it. Those are different screens, and they produce different names.

1 · Half-time delivery: who is actually on pace

The cleanest test at the halfway mark is not year-on-year growth — comparisons flatter whoever had the weakest base. It is first-half profit as a share of the company's own full-year plan. A business at ~50% is on pace against a target its own board signed off on.

Three readings worth separating:

  • The banks are boringly on pace. TCB 48.9%, VPB 45.7%, MBB ~46.8%. Nobody is

sprinting, nobody is broken. VPBank's 45.7% deserves the most credit because its plan was the sector's most aggressive — 45.7% of a very large number.

  • VPBankS's 42% is not what it looks like. Profit quadrupled year on year, but

₫3.46tn of the first half came from fair-value marks on its own securities book, not from customer fees or margin lending. Marks reverse. Fee income does not. The headline is the best in the sector; the quality of it is mid-table.

  • Vietcap at 29% is the outlier that matters. Against a plan targeting +41% for

the year, a first half at 29% requires a second half roughly 1.7× the first. That is possible — the FTSE upgrade lands in its strongest franchise — but it is now a forecast, not a trend.

The middle row is the one worth dwelling on. Right answer for the wrong reason is not a win — it is a warning that the model driving the answer is broken and will mislead next time. Anyone who bought the quota-winner screen made money in the first half. Anyone who bought it because they believed margins would compress now holds a position for a reason that has stopped being true, and will not notice until the reason starts mattering again.

3 · The funding picture behind the margins

Margins recovered at the leaders because deposits got cheaper for them specifically, not because policy loosened. It did the opposite: through the week of 20–24 July the State Bank of Vietnam had net-withdrawn roughly ₫330tn since early February, held its open-market rate at 4.5%, and let the central exchange rate climb to 25,283 — an eleventh consecutive weekly rise, essentially at the all-time high.

So the system-wide funding backdrop is tightening, and margin relief is not coming from the central bank. It is coming from franchise: the banks with genuine current- account balances pay less for money than the banks that buy deposits at the margin. That gap widens when liquidity is drained, which is exactly the environment now.

The practical screen this implies: stop ranking banks by quota awarded, and start ranking them by CASA ratio and the trajectory of deposit cost. Quota tells you how much a bank is allowed to lend. Funding tells you what it earns by doing so.

4 · What the second half actually hinges on

Three dated things, in order of how much they move the sector:

  1. 21 September — FTSE Secondary Emerging upgrade takes effect. Roughly US$1.5bn

of passive flow, phased through to late September. Banks and brokers are the liquid, foreign-room-available names that passive money must buy. This is the single largest scheduled flow event in the Vietnamese market this year.

  1. Late July — the Q2 filings still outstanding. Several large caps had not filed

as of writing, including MB Bank. Estimates are standing in for actuals across a meaningful share of the sector's profit.

  1. Deposit-rate direction into Q4. If the SBV keeps draining while credit demand

stays firm, deposit competition intensifies and the CASA gap becomes the whole story for 2027 forecasts.

The December view — that 2026 is a stock-picker's year rather than an index year — holds up, and if anything the first half sharpened it. But the dispersion is not falling where it was expected to. It is showing up between banks and their own brokerage arms, where the same parent produces a bank compounding at a 15–21% ROE and a securities arm whose profits swing on mark-to-market. Investors buying "the Vietnamese financial sector" are, in practice, buying two different businesses with very different earnings quality.

The one number to carry into the second half: deposit cost, not credit quota. Every thesis above resolves through it.
