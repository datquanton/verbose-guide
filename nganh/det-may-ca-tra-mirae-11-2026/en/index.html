Textile and pangasius exporters: milestones, drivers and cushion

US tariffs are now set (12.5% from 24 Jul 2026), so the next test is cost: oil, cotton and raw fish prices rose again in 4Q26. Mirae Asset keeps Buy on five of six, but the cushion differs widely: VHC and MSH hold more cash than debt, while TNG's debt is nearly 1.7 times equity.

Key points

  • Weak demand, share held. Textile exports grew only 1.1% over seven months; US apparel imports fell 7.7% in 1H26. Vietnam still gained US market share to 22.4%, but buyers order more cautiously: as of July, TNG had orders only through 3Q26.
  • Cost decides 2027. Crude was near US$100 a barrel in early May and rose again in October-November; raw fish prices turned up from early 4Q26. Mirae Asset cut TNG's FY26 net profit to VND461.4bn and ANV's to VND742.3bn (−25.7%), and forecasts lower 2027 revenue at both VHC and ANV.
  • The cushion is book value and cash, not 2027 earnings. PPH trades below book (P/B 0.86x), VHC near book with more cash than debt, MSH with more cash than debt and a forecast ROE of 30.7%. TNG, STK and ANV (in 2027) are the thin balance sheets.
  • Targets may fall. Mirae Asset says it may raise required returns (13% for TNG and MSH, 15% for PPH) and has flagged changes to VHC's assumptions; STK's target dates from May 2026.
How to read: drivers, milestones, cushion

Underlying drivers are the few variables that set a company's earnings (volume, price, margin, funding cost). Milestones are dated, checkable events that show whether the thesis is still on track. Cushion is what limits the fall if the drivers disappoint: valuation, balance sheet, earnings already secured and dividends. The gap to target price is only one part of the cushion, and it means something only while the target is fresh.

1 · Industry: what drives earnings now

Textiles and garments

Neutral, maintained in the latest sector report (Aug 19, 2026): exports improved in 2Q26, but rising competition and inflation are likely to dampen demand in key markets for the rest of 2026. Longer term, supply chain diversification away from China supports both garments and fiber. Preferred names: TNG and MSH (garments), PPH (fiber).

  • Export value: textile product exports reached US$22.8bn in 7M26 (+1.1% YoY; 4M26: +1.6% YoY); fiber exports reached US$2.7bn (+11.1% YoY; 4M26: +8.1% YoY), with China taking 54.1% of fiber exports.
  • Market share by destination (early Aug 2026): Vietnamese garments held 22.4% of the US market (2025: 21.5%) but slipped to 17.7% in Japan (2025: 18.8%) and 24.8% in South Korea (2025: 26.4%), where Chinese apparel reached 42.5% in 7M26.
  • US demand and orders: US textile product and apparel imports fell 7.1% YoY and 7.7% YoY in 1H26 (1Q26: -11.9% and -11.6%). Brand inventories are low, but customers order more cautiously and suppliers compete for a shrinking market.
  • US tariffs: a 12.5% Section 301 tariff on Vietnamese goods took effect on July 24, 2026 (10-12.5% on 59 other countries), below the proposed reciprocal tariffs. Broad tariffs raise US prices without changing competition among exporters; further tariffs are expected.
  • Input costs: crude oil was around US$100 per barrel in early May 2026 (up over 60% YTD), eased by early August, then high again in Oct-Nov 2026. Cotton: about 82 cent/lb in early May, up over 30% since Hormuz closed.

Pangasius (seafood exports)

Neutral (27 Aug 2026 sector report). Long-term prospects are viewed positively, but the short-term outlook is conservative: inflation concerns, rising US tariffs and anti-dumping duties and tougher competition weigh on exporters. After POR21, US prospects narrowed for most exporters except VHC; China becomes the key market, with price competition likely.

  • Export value: 7M26 pangasius exports reached US$1.3bn (+10% YoY), slowing from +17% YoY in 1Q26 (US$513mn) and +12% YoY in 1H26 (US$1.1bn); July alone was US$203mn (+4% YoY).
  • Market mix, 1H26: China US$299mn (+35% YoY, 27% of export value), US US$167mn (-5% YoY), Brazil US$88mn (-6% YoY). Mirae expects steady EU and Brazil demand in 2026 but more competition as exporters divert from the US.
  • Input costs: raw pangasius was about VND35,000/kg in early May 2026 and fingerlings peaked near VND85,000/kg; both fell sharply in 2Q26, then raw fish prices rose again from early 4Q26. Soybean prices were stable in 2Q26.
  • US trade barriers: a 12.5% Section 301 tariff on Vietnamese goods from 24 Jul 2026; POR21 final anti-dumping duties (12 Aug 2026) rose sharply, for example ANV to US$0.84/kg from US$0.00/kg, against a Vietnam-wide rate of US$2.39/kg.
  • Europe and VEFTA: Europe is about 20% of VHC's and 10% of ANV's FY25 revenue. VEFTA sets 0% duty on fishery exports to EFTA, but these were only US$52.8mn in 2025 (0.5% of Vietnam's fishery exports): a modest gain.

2 · Cushion: price against target, and what sits behind it

Gaps to target are large here (+23% to +48% on recent targets) because prices fell: TNG −12% and ANV −9% since the latest report. The firmer cushion is the balance sheet: MSH and VHC hold more cash than debt, and PPH has book value of VND30,646 a share against a price of VND25,800. TNG has the largest gap among recent targets (+48%) but the highest leverage, so it depends most on 4Q26-1Q27 orders.

Target price 24,700 (26 Oct 2026) · price 16,700 · gap to target +48% · 9M26 profit ≈78.4% · Thin · P/E 4.5x · P/B 1.06x

9M26 revenue ran ahead of forecast (+21.3% YoY), so FY26F revenue was raised to VND9,800bn, but high fuel costs cut FY26F NPAT to VND461.4bn (+17.4% YoY). Diversified markets and clients keep the 2027 growth view positive.

Underlying drivers

  • Valuation: share price of VND19,000 against FY26F EPS of VND2,939 and BPS of VND16,494; market P/E is 12.9x. The printed FY26F P/E of 8.4x and P/B of 1.5x correspond to the target price, not the market price.

  • Financial items: 3Q26 financial income VND34bn (3Q25: VND116.2bn) without last year's FX gains, financial expenses VND80.7bn (3Q25: VND22bn); 9M26 financial income -49.7% YoY, expenses +69.7% YoY. 9M26 NPAT fell to VND1,013.1bn (-16.0% YoY).

Milestones

  • passed: 12 Aug 2026 POR21 final anti-dumping results raised duties for most Vietnamese exporters; Mirae's sector note singles out VHC as the exception whose US competitiveness is not reduced. VHC's rate was US$0/kg before the review (Vietnam-wide: US$2.39/kg).
  • 4Q26 9M26 NPAT of VND1,013.1bn is 67.2% of FY26F VND1,507.9bn (NPATMI forecast VND1,433bn). 4Q26 must supply about one third of the year, well above 3Q26's VND229.3bn; Mirae says profit assumptions may need adjusting.
  • 4Q26-1Q27 Raw fish prices began rising again in early 4Q26; Mirae expects end-market demand to stay relatively robust through late 2026 and 1Q27. The next update may raise the required return (13% now), which would lower the valuation.
  • FY26 Company guideline for 2026: net revenue VND14,000bn and NPATMI VND1,600bn, above Mirae's FY26F (VND13,675bn and VND1,433bn). 9M26 revenue reached VND9,763.7bn; 1H26 NPATMI was VND735.2bn.

Cushion

  • Balance sheet: end-2025 cash and equivalents of VND4,348.3bn against short-term debt of VND2,046.1bn and no long-term debt; total debt to equity 20.4% (2025) and 20.1% (2026F); interest coverage 22.2x (2026F).
  • The cash buffer shrinks in the model: 2026F cash falls to VND2,537.9bn after capex of VND1,700bn and a VND945bn equity buy-back line, then to VND1,356.7bn in 2027F as working capital absorbs VND1,659.8bn.
  • Valuation: the share price of VND48,000 sits near the 52-week low of VND47,500 and close to FY26F book value per share of VND45,077; at the target price FY26F P/E is 10.4x versus a market P/E of 10.8x.

What breaks the thesis: Raw fish prices keep rising while demand in key markets weakens, pushing gross margin further below the 16.8% FY26F (3Q26: 14.3%); adverse trade policy in the US, the largest market, or a higher required return in Mirae's model would cut the valuation.
