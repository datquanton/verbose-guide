Property, steel and construction: milestones, drivers and cushion

Mirae Asset rates seven of eight Buy, but only HPG's target is both recent and not flagged for a cut. Housing sales fell as rates rose, and the 4Q26 handover season decides 2026 results; HPG is the exception, with nine-month profit already at 108% of guidance.

Key points

  • Housing: rates have cut sales. Under Vietnamese accounting, revenue is booked at handover, so KDH's 3Q26 revenue fell 96% and NLG's nine-month NPAT-MI is only 17.8% of its target. Mirae Asset plans to lower forecasts for both KDH and NLG.
  • The property cushion is customer advances and land, not earnings. NLG holds VND977bn of net cash and VND3.19tn of buyer advances; KDH has VND1.86tn of advances but net gearing up to 56.1%; HDC's 443ha land bank is carried at cost, but its net debt to equity is 57.9%.
  • Steel: HPG runs at full capacity. 3Q26 sales volume rose 56% and the steel EBIT margin reached 16.8% despite dearer coking coal. The concern is 2027: with capacity full, profit may stall or dip (forecast VND25.7tn against VND26.1tn). Net debt to equity is forecast to fall from 49.1% to 29.4%.
  • Three targets are old. HDC (Feb 2026), VRE (Dec 2025) and GDA (May 2026) have no new report; their gaps to target reflect falling prices, not cushion.

How to read this. Underlying drivers are the few variables that set a company's earnings (volume, price, margin, funding cost). Milestones are dated, checkable events that show whether the thesis is still on track. Cushion is what limits the fall if the drivers disappoint: valuation, balance sheet, earnings already secured and dividends. The gap to target price is only one part of the cushion, and it means something only while the target is fresh.

Residential, industrial and retail property

The latest ratings are Buy on all five stocks (VRE and HDC not updated since Dec 2025 and Feb 2026), but the 3Q26 notes turn cautious: higher interest rates and weak housing demand cut presales, industrial land handovers lag, and Mirae plans to lower forecasts for KDH and NLG. The reports read here contain no formal sector rating.

Steel and construction

Buy on HPG (target VND30,000) and GDA (VND16,600, May 2026), Hold on CTD (VND71,200). Domestic steel demand is supported by public investment and a large residential construction backlog, and HPG's 3Q26 profit beat expectations. Concerns: HPG's capacity is already full before 2027, higher interest rates weigh on property and contractors, and coated steel faces export barriers and new capacity.

  • Domestic demand: construction steel consumption reached 7.6mn tons in 1H26 (+14.8% YoY), of which 6.9mn tons domestic (+19.3% YoY); HRC consumption including imports rose 13.1% YoY to 7.5mn tons, supported by public investment and the residential construction backlog.
  • HRC price: HPG's June 2026 contract price reached US$597/ton (+20.6% from US$495 in January); forward prices for Jul/Aug/Sep were then cut 2.2%/8.0%/10.2% from the June peak; November 2026 offers were revised up 3.5% from September levels.
  • Input costs: coking coal (28-30% of HPG's COGS) rose 23% QoQ in 2Q26 and 16% in 3Q26, while iron ore rose 1.4% QoQ in 2Q26; HPG's steel EBIT margin still reached 16.8% in 3Q26.
  • Trade barriers and coated steel: after anti-dumping duty on Chinese HRC (AD20), coated-steel makers such as NKG rely on local HRC; exports fell sharply (GDA 2025: -62% YoY; NKG 9M25: -52.9% YoY) while new domestic capacity raises competition.
  • Construction: CTD's backlog was VND70tn at end-FY26 (June 2026) after VND60.3tn of new orders, but 1Q27 orders fell 25% YoY; higher interest rates, material costs (construction steel +4-6% YoY, cement +8-10% YoY) and receivable collection are the pressure points.

What breaks the thesis: Softer property market conditions delaying the shophouse launches again (already postponed from 4Q25 to at least 2Q26) would remove the 2026-27 sales uplift; new malls taking longer to fill would pressure margins. Risks are as of Dec 2025.
