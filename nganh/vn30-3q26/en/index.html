VN30 3Q26: earnings drivers and trends across the 30 constituents

VN30 profit rose 80.5% year on year, but VIC and VHM account for 44.5 of those 80.5 percentage points. The rest grew 38.0% and slipped on the previous quarter; banks’ provisions grew faster than their income.

Key points

  • Two companies dominate the headline. Net profit to parent of the 30 companies was 165.0tn (+80.5%). VIC contributed 23.8 and VHM 20.7 percentage points, both off a very low 3Q25 base. Excluding the two: +38.0% year on year, −1.3% on 2Q26.
  • Growth is broad, but the quarterly pace has weakened. 27 of 30 companies earned more than a year earlier, with median growth of +26.0%. Against the previous quarter, profit of the 13 banks was −6.6%.
  • Commodity margins are the second-largest driver. BSR: gross margin 2.9% → 16.2%, contributing 8.8pp. HPG: revenue +70.9%, gross margin 16.7% → 19.2%, contributing 4.6pp.
  • Banks: income up, provisions up faster. The 13 banks in the basket: pre-provision profit +28.0%, provision expense +46.7%, pre-tax profit +20.3% year on year. Against 2Q26, provisions +19.6% and pre-tax profit −6.1%.
  • Prices moved against earnings. 20 of 30 stocks fell in 3Q26 and 23 of 30 are down year to date; the VN30 is −9.4% while nine-month profit is +66.6%. The index P/E fell from 15.6x to 10.8x.

1 · The aggregate: the last eight quarters

Observed: The step-up in non-bank profit began in 4Q25, not in 3Q26. Nine-month profit: VN30 +66.6%; excluding VIC and VHM +37.1%; banks +19.1%.

Reading: The basket total double-counts (VHM is a subsidiary of VIC, and the basket holds other listed parent–subsidiary pairs), so it serves for comparison across periods, not as a measure of the size of profit. The “excluding VIC, VHM” line shows the rest still growing but flat over the last two quarters.

2 · Who produced the growth

Observed: Of the 80.5 percentage points: VIC and VHM 44.5; BSR and HPG 13.4; the 13 banks 13.2; the other 13 companies 9.3.

3 · Drivers by group

3.1 Property handovers: VHM and VIC

Observed: VHM: net revenue 16.4 → 85.4tn, gross margin 15.0% → 60.3%. Financial expenses in 3Q26 were 24.4tn, of which interest 4.5tn; the previous quarter’s were 5.2tn. Borrowings 141.5 → 240.6tn; nine-month operating cash flow 181.1tn.

Observed: VIC: net revenue 39.1 → 150.6tn; gross profit swung from a loss of 7.3tn to a profit of 49.4tn. 3Q25 profit rested on 33.5tn of financial income. Other income in 3Q26 was 4.7tn; interest expense 11.0tn; borrowings 389.9tn.

Reading: Both companies’ 3Q26 profit came from operations (gross profit), unlike 2025 when financial income did the work. Two items the data cannot explain: about 19.9tn of non-interest financial expenses at VHM in 3Q26, and VIC’s other income. The statement notes need reading. On the sales outlook, see Real estate, steel and retail 3Q26.

3.2 Commodities and margins: BSR, HPG, GAS, GVR

Observed: HPG: revenue 36.4 → 62.2tn; interest expense 812bn → 1,774bn; nine-month operating cash flow 8.8 → 20.9tn, capex 23.9 → 14.6tn. Nine-month profit includes 5.9tn of financial income booked in 1Q26.

Observed: BSR: gross profit rose 8.6tn on revenue +68.8%. GAS: gross margin 11.5% → 15.7%, profit −21.9% on the quarter. GVR: other income 1,998bn, equal to 45% of pre-tax profit, while revenue was only +2.3%.

Reading: HPG is growth from new capacity: revenue up, capex down, operating cash flow up, at the cost of interest expense that has doubled. BSR is growth from the refining margin, which swings widely: its gross margin was under 3% a year ago. The two differ in durability although both sit under “commodities”. For steel see Real estate, steel and retail 3Q26.

3.3 Banks: the 13 in the basket

Observed: Net interest income +19.3%, net fee income +75.2%, operating expenses +13.2%, provisions +46.7%. VCB: net interest income rose 5,729bn but provisions rose 5,409bn, leaving pre-tax profit flat. BID: provisions fell 1,545bn, about half of its pre-tax profit gain.

Reading: Income was enough to absorb higher funding costs; what decided the quarter was provisioning, which differed widely between banks. Margins, bad debt and valuation for all 28 listed banks are in Banks 3Q26.

3.4 Consumer and retail: MWG, MSN, MCH, VNM, SAB

Observed: MWG: revenue +32.0%, gross margin 18.8% → 21.7%, net profit to parent +74.7%; inventories 24.1 → 35.1tn. MSN: revenue +74.7% and +31.5% on the quarter, gross margin 30.4% → 28.2%. VNM +17.3%, MCH +15.1%, SAB −9.4%.

Reading: Modern retail is growing revenue and widening margin at once, a more durable pattern than growth from a single income item. MSN’s one-quarter jump in revenue came with a lower gross margin; the data does not show the cause (a change in consolidation scope or in sales mix are both possible), so it should not be extrapolated. Staples grew by single digits to the mid-teens, as the industry’s nature suggests.

3.5 Technology, aviation, brokers: FPT, VJC, SSI, TCX

Observed: FPT: revenue −20.1% but net profit to parent +11.8%; share of associates’ profit 131bn → 797bn, equal to 26% of pre-tax profit. Revenue stepped down from 1Q26 (20.2 → 12.5tn). VJC: financial income of 464bn exceeds pre-tax profit of 410bn. SSI −23.7%, TCX +7.9%.

Reading: FPT’s lower revenue is a change in consolidation scope: by the company’s own disclosure it stopped consolidating FPT Telecom from 1 Jan 2026, and on a comparable basis nine-month revenue rose 12.4% and pre-tax profit 17.0% (FPT’s 9M 2026 earnings report; not a figure from this page’s data source). The stock is down 42.3% year to date, more than the slowdown in profit. For the two brokers, 3Q25 was a high base for investment income; see Securities sector 3Q26 (Vietnamese).

4 · Earnings quality: items outside core operations

The items below are identified by their size against the quarter’s pre-tax profit. The data source has no statement notes, so the nature of each item is unconfirmed.

  • GVR: other income 1,998bn = 45% of 3Q26 pre-tax profit; the two previous quarters were 1,038bn and 1,057bn.
  • VIC: other income 4,665bn = 16% of pre-tax profit; 2Q26 carried 23.0tn of financial income.
  • HPG: 1Q26 financial income of 5,938bn = 55% of that quarter’s pre-tax profit; the nine-month growth of +103.5% includes it.
  • FPT: share of associates = 26% of pre-tax profit after the change in consolidation scope. MSN: 29% of pre-tax profit from associates.
  • VJC, VPL, VRE: financial income equal to 113%, 106% and 49% of 3Q26 pre-tax profit.
  • STB, LPB: net fee income jumped (673bn → 4,255bn; 853bn → 3,395bn) while net interest income fell; the source of the fees is not in the data.

6 · How the market responded

Observed: 12 stocks have nine-month profit up more than 20% and a lower price year to date: BID, CTG, HDB, HPG, MBB, MSN, MWG, VCB, VNM, VPB, VPL, VRE. VN30: P/E 15.6x → 10.8x, P/B 2.41 → 2.17.

Reading: The fall in multiples is broad, not confined to one sector, so it is hard to attribute to individual company stories. It fits two things that can be observed: a higher level of interest rates (see the macro page) and quarterly profit that has stopped rising outside the two large names. A low multiple is not by itself a reason to buy: where profit depends on commodity margins or handover timing, a low P/E tends to appear near peak earnings.

Method and sources

  • Source: FiinQuantX, pulled 13 Nov 2026. Consolidated single-quarter statements; the four quarters of 2025 sum to the 2025 annual statement within 1% for 28 of 30 companies (GVR differs by 1.2–1.8%; SSI by 2.6% because of one line of profit allocated to funds). 3Q26 statements are unaudited for 28 of 30.
  • Basket: the 30 current constituents, applied back to earlier quarters; the source gives no history of constituent changes.
  • Contribution: each company’s change in net profit to parent divided by the basket’s total in 3Q25. PBT bridge: the difference in each income-statement line between 3Q26 and 3Q25.
  • Double counting: the basket total adds listed parents and their listed subsidiaries; the page only adds an “excluding VIC and VHM” figure and does not re-consolidate.
  • Prices and valuation: adjusted closing prices; the data provider’s P/E and P/B; comparison dates 31 Dec 2025, 30 Jun 2026, 30 Sep 2026, 12 Nov 2026.
  • Not in the data: statement notes, the make-up of other income and financial income, the reasons for changes in consolidation scope. The FPT Telecom fact comes from FPT’s own disclosure and is marked where used.
  • Reading convention: “Observed” is verifiable from the data; “Reading” is the author’s judgment.
