Banks 3Q26: profit, margins, asset quality and valuation

Pre-tax profit of the 28 listed banks rose 21.3% year on year but fell 4.1% on the previous quarter: margins held although funding costs rose at 27 of 28 banks, while provision expense rose 33.4% and bad debt edged up to 2.11%.

Published 13 Nov 2026 · Coverage: 28 listed banks (HOSE, HNX, UPCoM), market cap VND2,597tn · Data: consolidated single-quarter statements 3Q25–3Q26; closing prices 12 Nov 2026 · Source: FiinQuantX

Key points

  • Income still grew well; quarterly profit fell because of provisions. Total operating income was +21.2% and pre-provision profit +24.9% year on year. Provision expense was +33.4% year on year and +16.6% on the quarter, so pre-tax profit was −4.1% on 2Q26. 21 of 28 banks earned more before tax than a year earlier.
  • Margins held because lending yields rose with funding costs. Funding costs rose at 27 of 28 banks on the quarter (median 5.03% → 5.35%), and asset yields rose at 26 of 28 (median 7.23% → 7.67%). Median NIM is 2.67%, unchanged on the quarter and below 2.85% a year earlier.
  • Asset quality worsened broadly. NPLs went from 2.02% to 2.11%, rising at 23 of 28 banks. Group-2 loans went from 1.37% to 1.57%. Coverage fell at 21 of 28 banks despite higher provision expense.
  • Fee income jumped, but two banks account for much of it. Net fee income was +76.7% year on year, an increase of 13.0tn; STB and LPB alone contributed 6.1tn (47%).
  • Nine-month results sit at the top of the start-of-year forecasts, yet valuation fell. Nine-month pre-tax profit rose 20.2%, against the research houses’ forecast range of +16–20% for full-year 2026. The sector P/B fell from 1.63x at end-2025 to 1.30x.

Observed: The quarterly cost-to-income ratio went from 31.8% to 29.7%: operating expenses rose 13.4%, slower than income. The three state-owned banks (VCB, BID, CTG): net interest income +26.1%, provisions +45.1%; the other 25 banks: +17.5% and +27.4%.

Reading: Operating leverage is working in the banks’ favour (income growing faster than costs). The quarter’s weakness sits entirely in the provision line. Banks tend to provision more toward year-end, so 4Q26 is the quarter to watch to learn whether 3Q26 provisioning was a peak or a start.

2 · Margins and funding

Observed: Net interest income was +20.3% on customer loans +17.0%. Customer deposits were +15.5%; valuable papers issued +42.7%, to VND2,267tn. CASA went from 21.0% to 19.4%, falling at 16 of 28 banks on the quarter. In 3Q26 deposits grew 4.9%, faster than loans (3.3%).

Reading: The chain that can be observed: loans grew faster than deposits through 2025 and the first half of 2026 → banks paid more for deposits and issued more valuable papers → funding costs rose at almost every bank. NIM did not fall further because lending rates were repriced alongside; system data point the same way (average lending rate +1.90pp, deposit rate +1.50pp, see Vietnam macro 11/2026). The risk to NIM is therefore not the current trend but the case where lending rates are held down while funding costs keep rising. Banks with high CASA (VCB, MBB, TCB) have visibly lower funding costs and are less exposed.

3 · Asset quality

Observed: Loan-loss reserves grew 2.4% in the quarter while coverage fell from 78% to 74% (a year earlier: 84%): bad debt is growing faster than reserves. VCB: provisions 776bn → 6,184bn, NPL 0.61% → 0.71%. STB: NPL 7.33%, coverage 35%. BID: provisions down 25.0% year on year while coverage fell from 95% to 73%.

Reading: Three things to read together. First, against the previous quarter asset quality worsened at most banks; against a year earlier the picture is mixed because 3Q25 was also a high-NPL quarter, so a quarterly figure should not be paired with an annual one. Second, fast credit growth flatters the NPL ratio (the denominator grows first, bad debt forms later). Third, profit that grows because provisioning falls while coverage declines is less durable than profit that grows on income. The improvement to look for is group-2 loans that stop rising and coverage that stops falling.

4 · Fee and non-interest income

Observed: STB: net fee income 673bn → 4,255bn while net interest income was −33.6%. LPB: fees 853bn → 3,395bn, net interest income −7.4%. Excluding the two, sector fee income rose 44.6%.

Reading: When fees jump while interest income falls at the same bank, the likelier explanations are income recognised on a different line or a non-recurring item, not a service business that multiplied in a year. The data has no notes to confirm it; fee growth for the rest of the sector is the more reliable gauge.

5 · Dispersion and valuation

Observed: The sector trades at 1.31x book on an annualised quarterly ROE of 17.4%. The sector price index was −3.8% in 3Q26 and is −0.4% year to date; P/E 10.1 → 7.8. STB trades at 2.03x book with an ROE of 2.5%; SHB at 0.66x with an ROE of 18.6%.

Reading: Profit growing about 20% while prices are flat to lower means the market is discounting asset quality and funding costs, not income. The ROE–P/B relationship holds in direction for most banks; the outliers (a high P/B on a low ROE, or the reverse) usually carry their own story such as restructuring or shareholder change, which quarterly numbers cannot explain. The 13 banks in the VN30 are covered in VN30 3Q26.

6 · Against the start-of-year forecasts

Observed: Banks 2026 on this site consolidates the research houses’ forecasts: sector pre-tax profit up 16–20% in 2026. After nine months, pre-tax profit of the 28 listed banks is 312.9tn, up 20.2%; total operating income is up 18.4%.

Reading: Nine-month results are at the top of the forecast range, but the composition differs from what was expected: income is higher, and so are provisions. Because the fourth quarter is usually the heaviest for provisioning, the full-year figure still depends on a line that 3Q26 has just shown to be accelerating.

Method and sources

  • Source: FiinQuantX, pulled 13 Nov 2026. Consolidated statements of the 28 banks in the ICB “Banks” group; income statements are single quarters, balance sheets period-end. 3Q26 statements are unaudited.
  • Checks: the accounting identities (income − costs = pre-provision profit; − provisions = pre-tax profit; assets = liabilities + equity) hold on all 252 bank-quarter rows; total net profit matches an independent query.
  • Provider ratios: NIM, asset yield, cost of funds and ROE are trailing 12 months, not the single quarter; the provider’s balance-averaging gives a lower NIM than the usual method for fast-growing banks, so it suits ranking better than comparison with bank-reported figures. CASA runs about 1–3pp below bank-reported figures.
  • Computed ratios: sector NPL, group-2, coverage and CASA are aggregated from each bank’s ratio times its balance; credit cost = quarterly provisions × 4 / average loans; quarterly CIR = operating expenses / total operating income; annualised quarterly ROE = net profit to parent × 4 / average equity.
  • Credit: customer loans on the balance sheet, excluding corporate bonds, so it differs from the State Bank’s credit definition. Not in the data: the capital adequacy ratio (CAR), detailed loan-classification notes, the make-up of fee income.
  • Start-of-year forecasts: the +16–20% range is from “Banks 2026” on this site, which consolidates published reports of brokers and rating agencies.
  • Reading convention: “Observed” is verifiable from the data; “Reading” is the author’s judgment.
