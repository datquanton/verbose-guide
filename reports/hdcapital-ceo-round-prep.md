# HDCapital — CEO Round Prep (30 minutes)

**Built:** 31 July 2026 · Companion to `buyside-analyst-interview-prep.md` and `vn30-2q26-review.md`

## 0. Verify before you walk in

Three things I could not confirm from open sources. Do not bluff any of them:

1. **The CEO's name.** HDCapital's site lists a CEO / board member with 15+ years in banking, finance, securities and fund management, formerly **Deputy CEO of Eurocapital Securities and HD Securities (HDBS)**, holding an SSC Fund Management Certificate and an **MBA from University of Technology Sydney**. The name isn't in search results. Get it from your recruiter or the invite — walking in without it is a bad look, and guessing is worse.
2. **AUM and fund performance.** Not publicly disclosed in anything I could reach. Ask the recruiter, or ask the CEO directly (it's a legitimate question).
3. **Which fund/desk the role sits on.** My strong inference is HDBF (below), but confirm.

## 1. Who you're meeting, and what the house actually is

**HDCapital (CTCP Quản lý Quỹ HD)** — licensed by the SSC on **24/11/2008** with VND 50bn initial charter capital, renamed from *CTCP Quản lý quỹ Đầu tư Chứng khoán Liên Minh Việt Nam* in **January 2022**. It is the fund-management arm of the **HDBank financial ecosystem** — HD Financial Group, ultimately inside **Sovico** (Nguyễn Thị Phương Thảo).

**Products:**
- **HDBOND** — high-yield bond fund, positioned for capital preservation with returns above 12-month deposit rates
- **HDBF (HD Banking and Finance Equity Fund)** — **open-ended equity fund, SSC certificate 17/04/2026**, investing selectively in leading banking and financial-services companies and businesses benefiting from the development of Vietnam's financial system. Actively managed, adjusted across market cycles, fundamental analysis combined with a macro overlay.

**The single most important fact for your prep: the newest flagship is a banking & financials sector equity fund, launched three months ago.** If you are hired as an equity analyst, the odds strongly favour you covering banks and financials. Your VN30 bank work is directly on-point — bring it.

**Chairman: Vũ Hữu Điền** (b. 1972). **24 years at Dragon Capital (2000–2024)** — joined as senior analyst, became Corporate Finance Director and Portfolio Investment Director, co-managing three funds with **over USD 1bn**. Then Chairman of VPBankS (July 2024) and concurrently CEO (Nov 2024), before stepping down from both. Now **Chairman of both HD Securities and HDCapital**, and **Senior Advisor to Sovico Group**. Education: University of Finance & Accounting HCMC; Master's from Solvay Brussels School.

You are meeting the CEO, not the Chairman — but the Chairman's pedigree tells you the house's intended standard. **This is being built to a Dragon Capital bar.** Prepare accordingly.

**The group is in aggressive build-out, and this is your "why us" material:**
- **HD Securities (HDS):** Q2/2026 profit **~4× YoY**, joining the VND 1,000bn+ profit club; capital raise running Q2–Q4 2026 to ~**VND 11,180bn** charter capital; became an **HDBank subsidiary**; holds **15% of HDEX** (digital asset exchange); won ABF Awards 2026 *Securities House of the Year – Vietnam*
- **HDBank (HDB):** 2026 target PBT **>VND 30,000bn (+41%)**, total assets **>VND 1,194,000bn (+28%)**, **ROE above 25%** — among the highest in the system; Q2/2026 PBT ~VND 6,800bn (+44%)
- Strategy pillars: funding-cost optimisation, non-interest income, digital, and international expansion via the **Vietnam International Financial Centre (VIFC)**

**Read:** HDCapital is the smallest piece of a group scaling hard, with **captive distribution** through HDBank's branch network and HDS. You would be joining a build, not a steady state. Say that back to them — CEOs hire builders for builds.

## 2. What a 30-minute CEO round is actually testing

Round 1 tested whether you can analyse. **The CEO round tests four different things:**

1. **Judgment and ownership** — can you make a call and live with it
2. **Commercial awareness** — do you understand that AUM, distribution and client trust pay your salary
3. **Representability** — would they put you in front of an institutional client or a bank distribution partner
4. **Longevity and motive** — why *this* firm, and will you still be here in three years

They are **not** re-testing your modeling. Do not try to prove technical depth by volume; prove it by precision — one sharp number beats five vague ones.

### Time budget

| Minutes | What's happening | Your job |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Small talk, "tell me about yourself" | 90-second narrative, land it clean |
| 3–12 | Their questions: why us, why buy-side, background probes | Be specific, be brief, leave hooks |
| 12–22 | "Pitch me something" / market view | One pitch, two minutes, then defend |
| 22–28 | Your questions | Ask about the build, not the perks |
| 28–30 | Close | One sentence on why you want it |

**Discipline rule:** in 30 minutes you get roughly **six substantive exchanges**. Do not spend two of them on your CV. Answer in 60–90 seconds and stop talking — CEOs read over-talking as poor judgment about what matters.

## 3. "Why HDCapital?" — the answer that separates you

Weak answer: "It's a growing firm with a strong ecosystem and I admire the brand."

**Strong answer, built on three specifics:**

> "Three reasons. First, HDBF launched in April into what I think is genuinely the right environment for an active banking fund — Q2 showed the widest dispersion across Vietnamese banks in years. Vietcombank grew pre-tax profit 58% while Sacombank fell 44%, and the difference wasn't lending margin, it was credit cost. Sector NIM is stuck below 3%, so spread is no longer the differentiator. That's an environment where security selection actually pays, which is exactly what an active sector fund is for — a passive bank basket can't capture it.
>
> Second, the group is building rather than defending. HDS is up roughly 4× in Q2 profit and raising capital toward VND 11,180bn; HDBank is targeting over VND 30,000bn pre-tax with ROE above 25%. I'd rather join the part of that where the research process is still being built than a house where it's already set.
>
> Third — and this is personal — the standard here is being set by someone who spent 24 years at Dragon Capital running over a billion dollars. That's the bar I want to be measured against."

**Why this works:** it demonstrates you researched the *product* not just the company, it contains a genuine analytical insight (dispersion is what active management monetises), and it flatters the house's ambition without flattery.

## 4. Your pitch — tailor it to a long-only bank fund

**Critical adjustment from the generic prep: HDCapital runs long-only open-ended funds. Do not pitch a short.** Reframe as **overweight / underweight**. Pitching a short at a mutual-fund house signals you don't understand the mandate.

**Lead with a bank.** It's their flagship sector and your strongest material.

### Primary: Overweight VCB

> "Overweight Vietcombank. Consensus going into Q2 was that VietinBank would overtake it as the system's profit leader — most brokers modelled that. It didn't happen: VCB printed Q2 pre-tax profit of about VND 17,400bn, up roughly 58%, on net interest income up about 35%, taking H1 to around VND 29,200bn.
>
> But the beat isn't the thesis. The thesis is *how* it grew. In a quarter where sector NIM is below 3% and the entire spread across banks came down to credit cost, VCB grew NII 35% while holding NPLs under 1%. It generated sector-leading growth without buying it with provisions — the exact inverse of Sacombank, which cut profit 44% with provisions at 5.5× and a bad-debt book that still *grew* VND 6,500bn in the quarter.
>
> In a sector fund, that's the pair trade in long-only form: overweight the bank compounding without credit cost, underweight the one still recognising it."

**Then the defense you must survive — VPBank.** The CEO will likely push you toward the higher-growth names, and VPB is the obvious one (Q2 PBT ~VND 11,000bn, +77%; H1 ~18,900bn, +68%).

> "VPBank's growth is real, and I'd hold some. But I'd size it smaller than the headline invites, for one reason: parent-bank credit growth of about 24.8% against a system average near 8.4%, concentrated in real estate up 44%. That's a very large relative-growth bet, and credit cost from it won't appear for several quarters. It's an earnings-now, risk-later profile. I'd rather be overweight the bank where I can already see the asset quality."

That answer shows you can hold two things at once — recognise a good result *and* price its risk. That is precisely the judgment a CEO is screening for.

### Backup, if asked for something outside banks: HPG

Capacity story mispriced as a commodity story — **70% of the full-year profit plan on 52% of revenue** means margin, not price. Plus the non-obvious point: **steel, aluminium and copper are exempt from the new US 12.5% tariff**, so it's a tariff-insulated industrial in a market that sold exporters indiscriminately.

## 5. The ecosystem question — handle this carefully

**HDBank (HDB) and Vietjet (VJC) are both inside the Sovico orbit.** Two live risks in the room:

**If asked about HDB.** Be positive but analytical, never sycophantic. Real substance: Q2 PBT ~VND 6,800bn (+44%), ROE above 25% among the system's highest, 2026 target PBT >VND 30,000bn (+41%). The honest analyst's caveat, which you *should* voice: part of the Q2 NIM strength is seasonal from upfront credit-line fee recognition, and a +41% profit target on +28% asset growth is ambitious against a 15% system credit cap. Saying that respectfully proves you'd be an analyst, not a cheerleader — which is the entire value of the seat.

**If asked about VJC.** Do not trash it and do not gush. The analytical fact is that Q2 revenue rose ~71% to about VND 30,500bn while after-tax profit was roughly VND 349bn — a sub-1.2% net margin. Frame it as a question, not a verdict: *"the question I'd want to answer is how much of the reported profit is flying versus sale-and-leaseback gains, and what the path to margin looks like against the fleet plan."* That is a legitimate analyst's framing that nobody can object to.

**If they ask how you'd handle covering ecosystem names** — this is a test of integrity, and the right answer is direct: *"The same way I'd cover anything else, and I'd expect the firm to want that. A fund that can't underwrite its own ecosystem honestly can't be trusted with client money in it. If I ever couldn't publish my real view, that's a conversation I'd want to have upfront rather than quietly compromise."* Do not raise this unprompted — but have it ready, delivered calmly.

## 6. Questions to ask the CEO

Ask about the build and the mandate. Two or three, not six.

1. **"HDBF launched in April. What does success look like for it at the twelve-month mark — is it AUM, is it performance versus the bank index, or is it something else?"** *(Shows you think in the firm's terms, not just the analyst's.)*
2. **"How much of the fund distribution runs through HDBank and HDS channels versus direct? I'm interested in how much that shapes what the fund can hold — flows into a captive channel behave differently."** *(Sophisticated. Very few candidates ask this.)*
3. **"With the Chairman coming from 24 years at Dragon Capital, what parts of that process are you deliberately importing, and what are you deliberately doing differently for a domestic-distribution house?"**
4. **"How does the analyst-PM relationship work here — do analysts carry attribution on their names, or is the book run centrally?"**
5. **"Where do you want HDCapital's AUM in three years, and what's the binding constraint on getting there — product, distribution, or investment capacity?"**
6. **"What separates someone who gets promoted here from someone who plateaus?"**

**Do not ask about:** salary, hours, or anything answerable from the website.

---

## 7. Landmines

1. **Pitching a short.** Long-only house. Overweight/underweight only.
2. **Not knowing HDBF exists.** It's their newest fund and probably your desk. Knowing it is table stakes; knowing *why its timing is interesting* is the differentiator.
3. **Flattering the ecosystem.** If you praise HDB or VJC without a caveat, you fail the independence test in one sentence.
4. **Quoting numbers you haven't re-verified.** Everything here comes from media reporting, not filings. Use "roughly," "around," "on the order of." A CEO who ran a billion-dollar book will catch a wrong number and remember it.
5. **Over-answering.** Six exchanges total. Respect the clock.
6. **Treating it as a technical round.** They already know you can analyse — round 1 established that. This round is judgment, commercial sense and fit.
7. **Having no answer to "where do you see yourself in three years."** For a build-out firm, the right shape is: deep sector ownership first, then broader responsibility as the platform scales. Signal that you intend to still be there.

---

## 8. Your 90-second narrative — structure

Do not recite your CV chronologically. Three beats:

1. **Where you come from** (20s) — one line on background and what you cover
2. **What you've built a view on** (40s) — a concrete analytical claim you own, ideally on banks. E.g. *"the thing I've spent most of this quarter on is why bank earnings dispersion widened so sharply — my conclusion is that credit cost has fully replaced NIM as the swing factor, and that changes which banks are worth owning."*
3. **Why this seat** (30s) — the HDBF/dispersion/build-out point from §3

Land beat 2 well and they'll spend the rest of the interview on it, which is exactly where you want to be.

## 10. Night before

- Confirm the CEO's name and title. Say it once, correctly, early.
- Re-verify the six numbers you actually intend to say out loud.
- Rehearse the 90-second narrative and the VCB pitch out loud, timed.
