# Buy-Side Equity Analyst — Interview Prep

**Built:** 31 July 2026 · **Market focus:** Vietnam (assumed from context — if the seat is regional EM/frontier, sections 1, 5, 6, 8 and 9 transfer unchanged; swap the stock work in sections 3–4)

**Same data caveat as the VN30 review:** figures below come from Vietnamese financial media reporting on filed statements, not the filings themselves (egress policy blocks direct fetching of cafef/vietstock/HOSE). Before you walk in, re-verify any number you plan to say out loud against a terminal or the company's IR page. **Never quote a precise figure in an interview you haven't personally verified** — being wrong on a number you volunteered is the single fastest way to lose a buy-side room.

---

## 1. What a buy-side interview actually tests

Sell-side interviews test whether you can *build and defend a model*. Buy-side interviews test whether you can *make a decision under uncertainty and own it*. Four things are being assessed, usually simultaneously:

| What they test | How it shows up | What a bad answer looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Do you have a variant perception?** | "Pitch me a stock" | Reciting consensus with a target price attached |
| **Do you know what would make you wrong?** | "What's the bear case?" | A token risk list you clearly don't believe |
| **Is your process repeatable?** | "How did you find it?" | "I read a broker report" |
| **Can you size and hold it?** | "How big? What do you do down 20%?" | No answer, or "I'd cut at -10%" with no reasoning |

The central buy-side question behind every other question is: **"Why is this mispriced, and why hasn't the market figured it out yet?"** A thesis without an answer to *why the inefficiency exists* is not a thesis. Valid answers: time horizon arbitrage (market focused on next quarter, you're underwriting three years), a structural misread (accounting artefact, sum-of-parts blindness), forced/indiscriminate selling, or genuine information edge from primary work.

## 2. The 90-second macro brief — memorize these

Vietnam is in an **unusual and genuinely interesting macro configuration right now**, and being able to characterise it crisply is worth more than any single stock fact.

**Growth is running hot:**
- GDP **+8.39% in Q2/2026**; **+8.18% H1** (vs 7.63% in H1/2025)
- Registered FDI **USD 34.65bn** as of 30/06, **+61% YoY**

**So is inflation, and rates have followed:**
- CPI **+5.25% YoY in Q2**; **+4.38% H1**, core **+4.12%**
- **Deposit rates have crossed 8%** — a new plateau, driven partly by USD/VND pressure
- Credit growth target **~15%**, deliberately below 2025's actual **19.1%**; SBV Directive 01 (09/01/2026) frames policy as "proactive, flexible, prudent"
- SBV is balancing three objectives at once: support growth, contain inflation, defend the currency, with the Fed still cautious

**The trade shock landed in July:**
- US **additional 12.5% tariff effective 24/07/2026**, replacing the temporary 10%
- H1 exports to the US ≈ **USD 86.5bn, ~⅓ of total exports**
- **Exempt:** electronics, machinery and components (~55% of exports to the US), civil aviation, medical, **aluminium/steel/copper**, some foods
- **Exposed:** textiles, wood, seafood

**The market:**
- VN-Index **1,744.66** (30/07), forward P/E **~13x** ≈ its 5-year average
- **FTSE Russell upgrade to Secondary Emerging confirmed, effective September 2026**, phased through **September 2027**. Passive inflow estimates: SSI ~**USD 1.5bn**; consensus total **USD 2–4bn** initially; bulls **USD 6–10bn**
- Foreigners have nonetheless sold **~VND 80,300bn YTD** (~VND 15,300bn in June alone)
- Q2/2026 aggregate listed profit **+23.5%** — the **weakest in four quarters**

### The one thing to have a view on

**Broker targets are unusually dispersed, and that dispersion is the interview conversation:**

| House | End-2026 VN-Index | Implied P/E | Earnings assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| MBS | **1,670–1,750** (i.e. flat-to-down from spot) | 12.5–13.0x | EPS +16–17% |
| SSI | 1,920 base / 2,120 bull | — | — |
| VNDIRECT | **2,014** | 13.5x | HOSE earnings **+21%** |

That is a ~20% spread on the same index, same quarter. **MBS is bearish because it thinks the new higher rate plateau drains market liquidity; VNDIRECT is bullish because it weights the FTSE upgrade and 21% earnings growth more heavily.** If asked "where's the market going," do not pick a number. Say: *the debate is entirely about whether the upgrade flow outweighs the domestic liquidity drag from 8%+ deposit rates, and here is which side I'm on and what data would change my mind.* That answer demonstrates you understand the mechanism rather than the consensus.

**A second live contradiction worth holding in your pocket:** Q2 bank commentary says sector NIM stays **below 3%**, while VNDIRECT's constructive bank call assumes NIM of **3.15%** with 17% credit growth. Both cannot be right. Noticing that on your own is exactly the kind of thing that makes an interviewer sit up.

## 3. Stock pitch framework

Structure every pitch to this shape. **Two minutes maximum for the opening**, then let them interrupt — buy-side interviewers interrupt on purpose to see whether you can defend the middle of your argument, not just the top.

1. **One-sentence recommendation** — ticker, direction, target, timeframe, conviction
2. **What the market believes** — state consensus fairly and specifically
3. **What I believe instead, and why** — the variant perception; this is the pitch
4. **Why the mispricing exists** — the inefficiency mechanism
5. **Three drivers**, quantified
6. **Valuation** — how you get to the number, and what's embedded in today's price
7. **Catalysts** with dates
8. **Risks and the disconfirming evidence** — name the thing that would make you exit
9. **Sizing** — position size and why

### Pitch A — Long HPG (Hoa Phat)

**Recommendation.** Long, core position, 12–18 month horizon, high conviction.

**Consensus.** A cyclical steel producer having a good year off a weak base; the market discounts it as a commodity price play.

**Variant perception.** This is a **capacity-cycle story mispriced as a commodity-cycle story**. Dung Quat 2's ramp is structurally lifting volume and mix, not just riding steel prices — and H1 margins came in ahead of plan, not just volumes.

**Evidence.** H1 revenue **VND 108,870bn (+47%)** and after-tax profit **VND 15,480bn (+103%)** — **70% of the full-year profit plan on 52% of the revenue plan.** That gap is the entire thesis: profits are outrunning volumes, which is margin, not price. Q2 alone: revenue 55,557bn (+53%), profit 6,424bn (+51%). Nearly 7m tonnes of crude steel in H1; HRC output +50% in the first five months.

**Second leg.** The Dung Quat rail-and-special-steel project (700k t/yr) is past 50% construction with product targeted from **Q2/2027** for national rail infrastructure — a domestic, policy-driven, non-export demand source with essentially no domestic competitor.

**The tariff edge — use this one.** Most people reflexively mark down Vietnamese exporters on the July tariff. **Steel, aluminium and copper are explicitly exempt from the additional 12.5%.** HPG is a tariff-insulated industrial in a market that has been selling exporters indiscriminately. That is a concrete, checkable, non-consensus point.

**Risks / what makes me wrong.** Chinese steel export pressure compressing regional HRC spreads; a construction slowdown if the rate plateau bites domestic property starts; execution slip at Dung Quat 2; and the honest one — **H1 was 103% profit growth off a soft base, so the 2027 comp is brutal.** I exit if HRC spreads compress two consecutive quarters *while* volumes are still rising, because that breaks the margin-not-price claim.

### Pitch B — Long VCB (Vietcombank)

**Recommendation.** Long, core, 12 months. The lowest-risk way to own Vietnamese banking beta.

**Consensus going into Q2.** Brokers widely modelled **VietinBank overtaking Vietcombank** as the system's profit leader.

**What happened.** VCB posted Q2 pre-tax profit of **VND 17,420bn, +57.9%**, on net interest income of **19,142bn (+35.2%)**, taking H1 to **29,222bn (+33.5%)** — comfortably retaining the top spot. CTG grew faster off a smaller base (H1 25,868bn, +36.7%) but did not overtake.

**Why it matters more than the beat.** In a quarter where sector NIM is stuck below 3% and the differentiator across banks was **credit cost, not lending margin**, VCB grew NII 35% while holding NPLs **below 1%**. It is generating sector-leading growth without buying it with provisions — the exact opposite of Sacombank. VNDIRECT's target is VND 67,100.

**Risks.** State-bank valuation premium is already substantial; a policy-driven credit reallocation could dilute the mix; if the 8%+ deposit plateau persists, even VCB's funding advantage narrows in absolute terms.

### Pitch C — Avoid / short STB (Sacombank)

Have a short or an avoid ready. Most candidates only bring longs, and it is the fastest available differentiator.

**Thesis.** Q2 after-tax profit fell **44% to VND 2,030bn** with provisions above **VND 5,000bn — 5.5× the prior year** — against a bad-debt book that *grew by VND 6,500bn* in the quarter, including Bamboo Airways exposure, and without the prior year's asset-liquidation gains.

**Why it's not already in the price.** The market has been treating this as a one-quarter clean-up. **Provisions rising 5.5× while NPLs are still building is not a clean-up; it is a recognition lag.** The bad-debt formation rate, not the provision charge, is the variable to track — and it is going the wrong way.

**What would change my mind.** Two consecutive quarters of declining NPL formation, or a credible resolution of the Bamboo exposure at a recoverable value.

### Pitch D — The "interesting one": FPT

Keep this as your answer to *"tell me about a stock you find genuinely hard."* It demonstrates intellectual honesty, which senior PMs weight heavily.

**The setup.** FPT has lost over **VND 114,000bn of market cap** from its peak and dropped out of the top-10 most valuable listed companies. Headline revenue looks like it collapsed: Q2 VND 13,789bn, H1 26,269bn.

**The accounting fact almost everyone gets wrong.** From **01/01/2026 FPT deconsolidated FPT Telecom** to equity-method accounting after the Ministry of Public Security acquired 50.2% (FPT retains 45.66%). Revenue and assets drop out of the consolidated accounts; the profit share stays. On a like-for-like basis Q2 attributable profit was **VND 2,568bn (+14%)**, H1 attributable **5,055bn (+14.1%)**, EPS 2,967 (+13.4%). Foreign IT services H1 revenue **18,902bn (+13.4%)**, 14 contracts above USD 10m, **new bookings +32.3%**.

*Tell-tale for the interview:* Vietnamese outlets have published "Q2 revenue −17%" and "H1 revenue +12.6%" **side by side**. They cannot both be right on the same basis. Spotting that inconsistency and explaining its cause is a strong signal.

**Why I'm not simply long it.** A 114,000bn de-rating is far larger than any deconsolidation optics justify — which means the market may be pricing something real: that agentic AI compresses the offshore IT services model structurally. Bookings +32.3% argues against that *so far*, but bookings are a lagging indicator of a demand-model shift. **The honest position is that this is a genuine two-sided debate and the H2 bookings trend arbitrates it.** Saying that is stronger than forcing a call.

## 4. Sector one-pagers

**Banks (13 of VN30).** Sector NIM below 3%; funding costs up; credit growth deliberately throttled to ~15% from 19.1%. **Credit cost has replaced NIM as the earnings swing factor.** Winners: VCB (+57.9% Q2 PBT, NPL <1%), CTG (H1 +36.7%), VPB (Q2 +77%, H1 +68%), TCB (record 9,670bn, fee income +37.6%), SHB (Q2 +59%), HDB (~+44%). Losers: STB (−44%), ACB (−12%, high securities-gain base), LPB (H1 −3%), VIB (Q2 −8.2%). **Watch VPBank specifically:** parent credit growth of +24.8% against a ~8.4% system average, concentrated in real estate (+44%), is the cleanest asymmetric risk in the sector — earnings today, credit cost in 4Q26–1H27.

**Property.** Dominated by one event: **Vinhomes H1 after-tax profit of VND 52,091bn (+379%)** — larger than any bank's H1 pre-tax profit in the country — at 86.8% of the annual plan in two quarters, with presales of 148,104bn (+119%) already contracted. VIC group H1 profit 20,375bn (4.5×) on revenue 222,300bn (+72.5%); VinFast delivered 128,662 vehicles (+78%). **Do not sum VIC/VHM/VRE/VPL — they're one consolidation perimeter.** The forward risk is the 2027 comp, not the current print.

**Consumer & retail.** The cleanest evidence of genuine demand recovery, because the growth is **volume-led not price-led**: MCH +~10% volume (H1 revenue 15,637bn, +14%), VNM's best quarter in five years (Q2 profit 3,184bn, +28%), MWG record quarter with gross margin expanding 20.1%→22.2% and Bach Hoa Xanh +31% in Q2. Laggard: SAB, where advertising spend ate the gross-margin gain. **Flag the financialisation:** MWG holds 66,600bn cash with 22,127bn in lending and bonds; SAB's cash is ~65% of assets. A rising share of "operating" profit at consumer names is interest income — a rate bet inside a consumer multiple.

**Securities.** TCX record Q2 PBT 2,097bn (+21%), assets >100,000bn; SSI Q2 PBT 1,511bn (+32%), margin book ~40,500bn. Profits are driven by **margin lending and IB, not brokerage fees** — so this is a leveraged bet on the upgrade/liquidity narrative, not a cash-flow business. Expected liquidity improvement to ~VND 34,000bn/session is the bull case.

**Industrials/resources.** HPG as above. GAS Q2 −24% YoY but +22% QoQ — pure base effect from a Q2/25 provision reversal. GVR's estimated +31% includes ~1,500bn of land compensation, so the growth is lower quality than the headline.

**Aviation.** VJC is the cautionary tale: revenue **+71%** to 30,499bn converting to just **VND 349bn** of profit — a sub-1.2% net margin. Volume without margin.

## 5. Technical question bank

**"Walk me through how you'd value a bank."**
Not DCF — you cannot cleanly separate financing from operations. Use **P/B against sustainable ROE** (justified P/B ≈ (ROE − g)/(COE − g)), cross-checked with a residual income or dividend discount model. Then explain what actually drives the answer *this quarter in Vietnam*: NIM is compressed and converging below 3%, so **the variance across banks is credit cost and non-interest income**, not spread. VCB earns its premium through sub-1% NPLs; STB's discount is a provisioning cliff.

**"Three ways to spot low-quality earnings growth."**
(1) Growth from one-offs or base effects — GAS's −24% is a prior-year reversal, ACB's −12% is a prior-year securities-gain base, GVR's +31% includes land compensation. (2) Profit growing while cash conversion deteriorates. (3) Accounting-perimeter changes — FPT's revenue "decline" is deconsolidation, not contraction.

**"Revenue up 71%, profit margin under 1.2%. What do you ask?"** (VJC)
Unit economics: RASK vs CASK ex-fuel, load factor, fuel hedge ratio, aircraft ownership mix (owned vs sale-leaseback), and how much of "profit" is sale-and-leaseback gains rather than flying. Then: is this a growth-investment phase with a credible path to margin, or structural cost inflation?

**"A company reports revenue down 17% and profit up 14%. Explain."**
Deconsolidation of a subsidiary to equity-method (the FPT case), a divestment, a shift from gross to net revenue recognition, or a genuine mix shift out of low-margin business. Ask for the like-for-like restatement before concluding anything.

**"How do you think about the FTSE upgrade?"**
Separate **passive** from **active** and **flow** from **fundamentals**. Passive is knowable and bounded (~USD 1.5bn per SSI; USD 2–4bn total initially), phased Sept 2026 → Sept 2027, so it is not a single-day event. Active flows are larger but discretionary and follow fundamentals. Note the tension: foreigners have sold ~VND 80,300bn YTD *despite* the upgrade being confirmed — which tells you the upgrade is at least partly priced, and that currency and rate expectations dominate flow decisions in the near term.

**"Why has the VN30 rebalance mattered less than people expected?"**
Because the passive money attached is small: four VN30 ETFs hold ~VND 10,000bn combined, and estimated rebalance buying around the MCH/TCX addition was **~VND 486bn net**. Index inclusion changes the *composition* of index earnings beta — swapping TPB and PLX for a branded-FMCG compounder and the country's most profitable brokerage — far more than it moves prices.

**"Position sizing?"**
Size on **conviction × liquidity × correlation**, not conviction alone. Have a real answer: e.g. core positions 4–6% where the thesis has multiple independent drivers and daily liquidity supports exit within N days; 1–2% for single-catalyst or thesis-in-progress ideas. Then the follow-up you must survive: **"It's down 20%. What do you do?"** The right answer is a process: *has the thesis broken, or has the price moved?* Name in advance the specific disconfirming evidence — for HPG, two consecutive quarters of spread compression with rising volumes. If that hasn't happened, you add; if it has, you exit regardless of price.

## 6. Behavioral answers to prepare

- **"Why buy-side, not sell-side?"** — Accountability. Sell-side output is judged on quality of argument; buy-side output is judged on P&L. Say you want the version where being wrong costs you and you find that out fast. Avoid the two clichés ("better hours," "I like investing").
- **"Walk me through your process for generating ideas."** — Have a real one with a funnel: screen → anomaly → primary work → thesis → sizing. Cite a concrete anomaly you actually noticed. Reading a broker report is not a process.
- **"Tell me about a time you were wrong."** — Pick one where you were wrong on *the thesis*, not on timing, and where you can state the specific evidence that changed your mind and what you changed in your process afterwards. Timing excuses read as not having learned anything.
- **"What do you read?"** — Be specific and short. Name primary sources (company IR, HOSE disclosures, GSO releases, SBV directives) before secondary ones. Naming only media is a red flag on the buy-side.

## 7. Questions to ask them

Ask things that reveal you're evaluating the *seat*, not just seeking the job:

- How is the analyst-to-PM idea flow structured — do analysts carry P&L attribution or only recommendations?
- What's the typical holding period, and how much turnover does the mandate tolerate?
- How do you handle liquidity constraints in a market this concentrated — what's the largest position you can realistically build in a mid-cap?
- How does the team think about the FTSE upgrade — as a flow event to trade or a re-rating to underwrite?
- What separates an analyst who gets promoted here from one who doesn't?

## 8. Traps to avoid

1. **Quoting precise numbers you haven't verified.** Say "roughly," "about," "on the order of" unless you are certain. Every figure in this document should be re-checked before it leaves your mouth.
2. **Pitching only longs.** Bring at least one short or avoid (STB).
3. **Summing consolidated entities.** Adding VHM + VIC + VRE + VPL profits double-counts. Interviewers use this exact trap.
4. **Confusing base effects with performance.** GAS, ACB, VPL and HPG all have base-effect distortions in their headline growth.
5. **Treating index inclusion as a flow event.** ~VND 486bn of rebalance flow is noise.
6. **Having no exit discipline.** "I'd hold for the long term" is not risk management.
7. **Presenting consensus as insight.** If your pitch could be assembled from one broker note, it isn't a pitch.
